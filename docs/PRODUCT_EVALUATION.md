# Product evaluation standards

Use this framework before committing material engineering effort and at major roadmap reviews. It is a decision aid, not a formula that guarantees success. Different industries and stacks are welcome.

## Evidence brief

Each product or engagement records:

| Dimension | Required question and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Users and buyer | Who experiences the problem, who pays, and who decides? |
| Validated problem | Which observed workflow fails? Separate interviews, measured behavior and assumptions. Record dates and evidence quality. |
| Existing alternatives | How do users solve it today? Why would switching be worth the cost? |
| Value | What measurable outcome improves? Define the baseline and experiment. |
| Repeatability | Is demand recurring across users, or primarily bespoke delivery? |
| Distribution | How can the team reach users and test demand without assuming channels work? |
| Economics | What could support recurring revenue after infrastructure, AI, support and acquisition costs? Keep sensitive numbers private. |
| Feasibility | Which integrations, data rights, capabilities and operational constraints need proving? |
| Risk | What security, privacy, reliability, accessibility and regulatory considerations apply? Seek qualified advice when required. |
| Strategic fit | Does it advance intelligent business infrastructure or produce transferable insight? |
| Differentiation | Which defensible advantage can be tested rather than merely asserted? |
| Exit conditions | What would cause continuation, revision, pause or cancellation? Who decides and when? |

## Decision stages

1. **Explore:** state assumptions, users and learning questions.
2. **Validate:** collect evidence of pain, willingness to adopt/pay and feasibility; use the smallest useful experiment.
3. **Build a bounded product:** accept scope and non-goals, release criteria and architecture. Record unresolved risks.
4. **Pilot:** test outcomes, usability, operability and economics with authorized users.
5. **Scale:** expand only when evidence supports repeatable value, reliability and sustainable operations.

At each review record the decision, evidence, accountable owner, date and next experiment. Do not label a problem validated just because code exists. Define product-specific targets with owner approval; do not invent metrics, customers, revenue or results.

## Client engineering

Evaluate funding contribution, recurring problem insight, reusable learning, contractual/IP boundaries and distraction from the product portfolio. Avoid promising a product roadmap to satisfy an isolated client request. Reuse only authorized generic patterns. Bespoke work is permissible; its classification and strategic rationale must be explicit.
